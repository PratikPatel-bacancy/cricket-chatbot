# Match Format

## Singles and doubles

Tennis is played either as singles (one player per side) or doubles (two players per side, sharing
the court with wider sidelines in play). Doubles has specific serving-order and receiving-order
rules (partners alternate serving each game, and alternate receiving within a game), but scoring
(points, games, sets) works the same as singles.

## Changing ends

Players change ends of the court after the first game and then after every odd-numbered game
total in the set (e.g. after the 1st, 3rd, 5th game, etc.), including a short break to sit down
after every second game once a set has been underway, per most tour rules.

## Grand Slam tournaments

The four Grand Slam tournaments are the Australian Open, the French Open (Roland Garros), Wimbledon,
and the US Open. Men's singles matches at Grand Slams are traditionally best-of-5 sets, while
women's singles matches are best-of-3 sets. Court surfaces vary: hard court (Australian/US Open),
clay (French Open), and grass (Wimbledon).

## Advantage set vs. final-set tiebreak

Historically, the deciding (final) set in Grand Slam matches was played as an "advantage set" —
no tiebreak, requiring a player to win by a 2-game margin even beyond 6-6, sometimes leading to
very long final sets. Most Grand Slams have since adopted a final-set tiebreak (with varying formats,
such as a first-to-10-points tiebreak at 6-6) to avoid excessively long matches.

## Coaching and time rules

On-court coaching rules vary by tournament (some allow limited coaching during changeovers, others
prohibit it entirely). Players are also subject to a shot clock between points (commonly 25
seconds) in most modern professional tournaments to reduce time-wasting.
