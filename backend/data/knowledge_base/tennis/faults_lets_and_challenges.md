# Faults, Lets, and Challenges

## Service faults

A serve is a fault if the ball: lands outside the diagonally opposite service box, touches the
server's own side of the net without going over, hits the server's body or clothing, or is hit
before tossing correctly (a player can let a tossed ball drop without swinging and it doesn't
count as a fault, as long as they don't strike it).

## Let

A "let" is called (and the point, or just the serve, is replayed) most commonly when a serve
clips the top of the net cord but still lands in the correct service box. A let can also be called
for other outside interference during a point, such as a ball rolling onto the court from an
adjacent match.

## Foot faults

If the server's foot touches or crosses the baseline (or, for a doubles serve, the extended
imaginary center-service-line/singles-sideline boundaries) before striking the ball, it is a foot
fault, counted the same as any other service fault.

## Hawk-Eye and electronic line calling

Many professional tournaments use camera-based ball-tracking (commonly branded Hawk-Eye) to
determine whether a ball landed in or out. In tournaments with player challenges, each player gets
a limited number of unsuccessful challenges per set (an extra one is often awarded in a tiebreak)
to request an electronic review of a line call; if the challenge is correct (the original call is
overturned), the player keeps that challenge. Some tournaments now use fully automated electronic
line calling with no human line judges and no player challenge system at all.

## Other common violations

- **Touching the net**: a player loses the point if they, their racket, or their clothing touches
  the net, net posts, or the opponent's side of the court while the ball is in play.
- **Double hit / carrying the ball**: hitting the ball twice in succession, or "carrying" it
  (prolonged contact rather than a clean strike), results in loss of the point if deemed
  deliberate or clearly outside a single continuous swing.
