# General Laws and Glossary

## The pitch and creases

- **Pitch**: The 22-yard (20.12m) strip between the two sets of stumps where the bowler delivers the ball.
- **Popping crease**: The line 1.22m (4 feet) in front of each set of stumps; batters must ground bat or body behind it to be "in their ground" and avoid being run out or stumped.
- **Bowling crease**: The line through the stumps, marking the back edge of the delivery area.
- **Return crease**: The side lines perpendicular to the popping and bowling creases, marking the width within which the bowler's back foot must land.

## Team composition

- Each team fields 11 players.
- A team can use up to 4 substitute fielders in some competitions (playing conditions vary), but substitutes cannot bowl, bat, keep wicket, or captain the side — they are for fielding only, unless a concussion substitute rule applies (which allows a like-for-like replacement who CAN bat/bowl, used specifically for a player withdrawn due to a diagnosed concussion).

## The toss

Before the match, the two captains toss a coin; the winner chooses whether to bat or field first (subject to any specific playing conditions, e.g. some competitions eliminate the toss and let the visiting team choose).

## Extras

Runs added to the batting team's total that are not credited to any individual batter: byes, leg byes, wides, no-balls, and penalty runs.

- **Bye**: Runs scored when the ball passes the striker without touching the bat or body and the batters run.
- **Leg bye**: Runs scored when the ball hits the batter's body (not the bat) and the batters run, provided the batter attempted a shot or tried to avoid the ball (an umpire will not award leg byes if the batter made no attempt to play or avoid the ball).

## Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) Method

A mathematical formula used to calculate a revised target score for the team batting second in a limited-overs match interrupted by weather or other stoppages, based on overs remaining and wickets lost, to keep the match fair when the full number of overs cannot be bowled.

## Super Over

A tie-breaker used in limited-overs cricket (mainly T20, and ODIs in some tournaments) when the match ends level: each team bats one over (six balls) with a reduced side (typically 3 batters, subject to competition rules), and whichever team scores more in their over wins. If still tied, further Super Overs may be played depending on the competition's rules.

## Common terms

- **Maiden over**: An over in which no runs are scored off the bat and no extras (except sometimes byes/leg byes depending on scoring convention) are conceded.
- **Duck**: A batter dismissed without scoring any runs.
- **Century / Half-century**: A batter scoring 100 / 50 runs in an innings.
- **Hat-trick**: A bowler taking three wickets with three consecutive deliveries (not necessarily in the same over, but must be consecutive balls the bowler delivers).
- **Nightwatchman**: In Test cricket, a lower-order batter sent in ahead of their normal batting position late in a day's play, to protect a recognised batter from having to face the last few overs before the close of play.
