# Ways of Getting Out (Dismissals)

Cricket's Laws (Law 30–39 area of the MCC Laws) define ten recognised methods of dismissal. LBW and stumping/run-out get more detail in their own sections; this document summarises all ten.

## Bowled

The bowler's delivery hits the stumps directly (or via the batter's bat or body, but not via another fielder) and dislodges at least one bail, or knocks a stump out of the ground. The batter is out regardless of whether they knew or intended it.

## Caught

A fielder (including the bowler or wicketkeeper) cleanly catches the ball, without it touching the ground, after it has touched the batter's bat or a hand holding the bat, before the ball becomes dead. The fielder must have control of the ball and their own balance/movement within the field of play (or, for boundary catches, satisfy the boundary-catch rule — see the boundary and catch rules document).

## LBW (Leg Before Wicket)

See the dedicated LBW document for the full five-condition test.

## Run Out

A fielder puts down the wicket (breaks the stumps with the ball, hand holding the ball, or thrown ball) while the ball is in play and the batter concerned is out of their ground (i.e., hasn't grounded their bat or body behind the popping crease). This can happen to either batter, at either end, while they are running between the wickets, and it applies regardless of what caused the ball to be live (missed shot, deflection, even a no-ball or wide, since run out is not protected against those).

## Stumped

The wicketkeeper breaks the stumps with the ball while the striker is out of their ground and not attempting a run (typically after missing or playing and missing a shot, stepping or overbalancing out of the crease). The bowler is credited with the wicket. A batter cannot be stumped off a no-ball.

## Hit Wicket

The striker is out if they dislodge a bail or knock a stump out of the ground with their bat, body, or equipment while playing a shot, setting off for a run, or in the act of avoiding being hit, after the bowler has entered their delivery stride. Cannot occur off a no-ball.

## Handled the Ball (now covered under Obstructing the Field)

Historically a separate law, deliberately touching the ball with a hand not currently holding the bat (without fielding-side consent) is now judged under "obstructing the field." Out even off a no-ball or wide.

## Hit the Ball Twice

A batter who deliberately strikes the ball a second time, other than for the sole purpose of guarding their wicket, or to return the ball to a fielder, can be given out.

## Obstructing the Field

A batter who deliberately obstructs or distracts the fielding side by word or action can be given out, at any point while the ball is in play.

## Timed Out

An incoming batter who fails to be ready to face a ball (or their partner ready to take strike) within a set time limit after the fall of the previous wicket (traditionally 3 minutes, reduced to 2 minutes under some modern playing conditions) can be given out timed out, credited against the incoming batter, without facing a delivery.

## Retired Out

A batter who retires for reasons other than injury, illness, or another valid reason, and does not have the fielding captain's consent to resume batting later, can be recorded as "retired out" if they do not return. This differs from "retired hurt/not out," where the batter can resume their innings later with the fielding side's consent, or automatically in many playing conditions.
