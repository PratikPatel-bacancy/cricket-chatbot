# Faults and Lets

## Common faults during a rally

A fault (resulting in loss of the rally to the opponent) occurs if:

- The shuttle lands outside the boundaries of the court (out of bounds), or fails to pass over the
  net, or passes through or under the net.
- A player touches the net or its supports with their racket, body, or clothing while the shuttle
  is in play.
- The shuttle is hit twice in succession by the same player, or is hit by a player and their
  partner successively (a "double hit").
- A player invades the opponent's court over the net with their racket (except a natural
  continuation of a stroke after contact, which is generally allowed), or invades under the net
  in a way that interferes with the opponent.
- A player deliberately distracts an opponent (e.g. shouting or making gestures).
- The shuttle touches a player's body or clothing.

## Service faults specifically

In addition to the general faults above, a service is also a fault if: the shuttle is hit above
the server's waist/rib height or with the racket head not pointing downward at contact, the
server's or receiver's feet are not properly positioned in their service courts, the server misses
the shuttle entirely when attempting to serve, or the serve doesn't land in the correct diagonal
service court.

## Let situations

Unlike a fault, a let means the rally is simply replayed with no point awarded to either side.
Common let situations include: the shuttle getting caught on the net after crossing over and
staying there, a broken shuttle occurring mid-rally, the receiver not being ready when the serve
is struck, or an umpire being unable to decide on a close call and choosing to replay the point.

## Racket and equipment checks

Rackets may be checked before or during a match for compliance with size and stringing
regulations, and shuttles must meet approved specifications for speed and construction.
