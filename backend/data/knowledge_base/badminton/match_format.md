# Match Format

## Event types

Badminton is played in five main disciplines: men's singles, women's singles, men's doubles,
women's doubles, and mixed doubles (one man and one woman per side). Court dimensions differ
slightly between singles and doubles — doubles uses a wider court (including the outer tramlines)
but a shorter serve-receiving depth at the back.

## Changing ends

Players/sides change ends after each game, and also during the third (deciding) game once the
leading side reaches 11 points, to equalize any environmental factors like air currents or lighting
across the match.

## Intervals and coaching

A 60-second interval is allowed when the leading side's score reaches 11 points within a game, and
a 2-minute interval is allowed between games. Players may receive coaching from their bench during
these intervals and between games, but not during continuous play within a rally sequence.

## Doubles service rotation

In doubles, only the receiving side can score a point when they win a rally on the opponent's
serve, at which point they also gain the right to serve next. Within a serving side, partners
rotate which one serves depending on whether their team's score is odd or even, and which service
court (left/right) they last served or received from — the exact rotation follows a fixed pattern
tied to the score, not free choice by the players.

## Continuous play

Play is expected to be continuous from the first serve to the match's completion, except for
permitted intervals, a suspension ordered by the umpire, or in exceptional circumstances (such as
recovering from injury within permitted limits).
