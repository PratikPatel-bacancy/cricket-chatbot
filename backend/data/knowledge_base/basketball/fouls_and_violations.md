# Fouls and Violations

## Personal fouls

A personal foul is illegal physical contact with an opponent, such as blocking, charging,
holding, pushing, or illegal use of hands. A player who commits 5 personal fouls in FIBA/college
rules (6 in the NBA) fouls out and cannot continue playing in that game. Fouls committed while
shooting result in free throws for the fouled player (2 for a shot inside the three-point line, 3
for a shot from beyond it, if the shot is missed; if the shot is made, it counts and one bonus free
throw is awarded).

## Team foul penalty ("bonus")

Once a team accumulates a set number of team fouls in a period (rules vary by league, commonly
5 in FIBA/NBA-style quarter systems), the opposing team shoots free throws for subsequent
non-shooting personal fouls instead of just getting the ball back, known as being "in the bonus."

## Technical and flagrant fouls

- **Technical foul**: for unsportsmanlike conduct such as arguing with officials, taunting, or
  delay of game, rather than illegal contact during play. Results in free throws for the opposing
  team plus possession.
- **Flagrant foul**: excessive or unnecessary contact. A flagrant foul results in free throws for
  the fouled team plus possession of the ball, and can lead to ejection depending on severity.

## Common violations (no physical contact, result in loss of possession)

- **Traveling**: moving one or both feet illegally while holding the ball without dribbling (e.g.
  taking too many steps without dribbling).
- **Double dribble**: dribbling with both hands simultaneously, or stopping a dribble and then
  dribbling again.
- **Backcourt violation**: once the offense has advanced the ball past halfcourt, they cannot
  legally send it back into their own backcourt.
- **Goaltending / basket interference**: blocking a shot on its downward flight above the rim
  level, or touching the ball while it's on the rim or within the imaginary cylinder above the
  basket.
- **Three-second violation**: an offensive player cannot stay in the opponent's free-throw lane
  ("the paint") for more than 3 consecutive seconds while their team has the ball.
