# Scoring and the Clock

## Scoring

- A field goal made from inside the three-point line is worth **2 points**.
- A field goal made from beyond the three-point line is worth **3 points**.
- A free throw (awarded after certain fouls) is worth **1 point**.

## The shot clock

The offensive team must attempt a shot that hits the rim (or scores) before the shot clock expires
— 24 seconds in the NBA and FIBA, 30 seconds in NCAA men's/women's basketball (rules vary slightly
by competition). If the shot clock expires without a legal attempt hitting the rim, possession
turns over to the other team. The shot clock resets on a made basket, a defensive rebound, certain
held-ball situations, and some other events depending on the exact league's rules.

## The game clock

A standard game is divided into periods: four 12-minute quarters in the NBA, four 10-minute
quarters in FIBA international play, or two 20-minute halves in NCAA college basketball. The clock
stops for fouls, violations, timeouts, and out-of-bounds balls, especially in the final minutes of
close games.

## Overtime

If the score is tied at the end of regulation, one or more overtime periods are played (typically
5 minutes each) until a winner is determined.

## Timeouts

Each team is allotted a set number of timeouts per game (varies by league and sometimes by half),
which can be called by a player or coach during a stoppage in play to rest, strategize, or slow the
opponent's momentum.
