# Game Structure

## Team composition

Each team has 5 players on the court at a time, with substitutions allowed during dead-ball
situations (fouls, violations, timeouts, out-of-bounds). Bench sizes and total roster limits vary
by league.

## Starting and restarting play

Games typically start with a jump ball (or, in the NBA, an opening tip) where the referee tosses
the ball up between two opposing players who try to tap it to a teammate. After made baskets, the
scored-against team simply inbounds the ball from behind their own baseline; other restarts (after
violations or fouls) are also done via inbound passes from out of bounds.

## Positions (informal, not enforced by rules)

Basketball doesn't formally assign positions in its rules, but common informal roles are: point
guard, shooting guard, small forward, power forward, and center — reflecting typical playing
styles and court responsibilities rather than a rule requirement.

## Winning the game

The team with the most total points when the game clock reaches zero (after all regulation periods
and any necessary overtime periods) wins. There is no tie in standard basketball rules — overtime
periods are played until a winner is decided.

## Common terms

- **Rebound**: gaining possession of the ball after a missed shot.
- **Assist**: a pass that directly leads to a teammate scoring.
- **Turnover**: losing possession of the ball to the opponent without a shot attempt (e.g. a bad
  pass, a violation, or a steal by the defense).
- **Double-double / triple-double**: reaching double digits in two, or three, of the major
  statistical categories (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks) in a single game.
