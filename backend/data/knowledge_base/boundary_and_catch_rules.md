# Boundary and Catch Rules

## Scoring a boundary

- **Four**: The ball touches the ground at any point before crossing the boundary rope/line after being hit by the bat (or, in some cases, off the batter's body counted as leg byes still cross for 4 runs to the extras/leg-bye tally, not the batter).
- **Six**: The ball clears the boundary rope/line without touching the ground inside the field of play after being hit by the bat.
- If the ball touches or crosses the boundary marker (rope, painted line, or boundary cushion) itself, or the ground beyond it, that determines the boundary — the exact boundary marker used is defined by the ground's playing regulations.

## The boundary-catch rule

A fielder can complete a catch even after jumping from outside the boundary, or while airborne over the boundary, as long as:

1. The fielder's first contact with the ball is completely within the field of play (inside the boundary line), while the fielder is not touching or grounded beyond the boundary.
2. The fielder does not touch the boundary rope/line or the ground beyond it with any part of their body while in contact with the ball or before completing control of the catch, until after the ball has been permanently released (thrown back in).
3. A fielder may jump from inside the boundary, catch or parry the ball while airborne over the boundary (even beyond the line, as long as they are still in the air and haven't touched the ground/rope), and then throw it back to a teammate who completes the catch inside the field — this is a legal "relay catch."
4. If the fielder catches the ball but then steps on or over the boundary while still in control of the ball (grounded), it is no longer a clean catch — runs are scored as if the ball had crossed the boundary (6 runs if it would have cleared the rope, or otherwise runs completed/in progress).

## Overthrows

If, after a fielder's throw, the ball goes to the boundary due to a misfield, deflection off a fielder, or hits the non-striker/helmet/other equipment on the field and runs to the boundary, the batting side is awarded the boundary runs (4) plus any completed runs the batters had already run before the throw. If the ball deflects off a fielder or the ground and reaches the boundary without a direct hit being involved in a run-out attempt, it typically counts as 4 overthrow runs added to the runs already completed.

## Short runs

If a batter fails to ground their bat or body behind the popping crease while completing a run (cutting the corner), the umpire can call it a "short run" — that run does not count, though any other completed runs in the same passage of play still count.
