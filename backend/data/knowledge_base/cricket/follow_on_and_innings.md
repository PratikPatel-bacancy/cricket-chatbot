# Follow-On and Innings Structure

## What the follow-on is

The follow-on is a rule that applies in multi-day (Test/first-class) cricket. If the team batting second scores substantially fewer runs than the team that batted first, the side in the lead can force the team that batted second to bat again immediately, rather than the leading team batting a second time first.

## Follow-on run margins

The captain of the side that batted first can enforce the follow-on if their team leads by at least:

- **200 runs** in a match of 5 days or more (standard Test match length).
- **150 runs** in a match of 3 or 4 days.
- **100 runs** in a two-day match.
- **75 runs** in a one-day match.

## Enforcing the follow-on

Enforcing the follow-on is the leading captain's choice, not automatic — even if the margin is met, the captain can choose to bat again instead (common if the pitch is expected to deteriorate, or their bowlers need rest).

## Innings structure by format

- **Test match**: Each team bats twice (two innings), over up to 5 days, unless the follow-on changes the order. The match can end in a win, a draw (if time runs out with the match unfinished), or a tie (extremely rare).
- **One Day International (ODI)**: Each team bats once, limited to a maximum of 50 overs per innings (fewer if reduced by weather, e.g. via the DLS method).
- **T20 International / T20 league**: Each team bats once, limited to a maximum of 20 overs per innings.
- **First-class cricket**: Similar to Tests but typically 3-4 days; follow-on margins for shorter matches apply as above.

## Declaration

In matches with unlimited overs (Tests, first-class cricket), the batting captain can declare their innings closed at any point, even if not all batters are out, usually to leave enough time to bowl the opposition out and force a win rather than run out of match time.

## Result types

- **Win**: One team scores more runs than the other and completes/dismisses the required innings within the match time.
- **Draw**: In unlimited-overs cricket, if time runs out before a result is reached.
- **Tie**: Both teams finish with the exact same number of runs after all required innings/overs are completed (different from "no result," which is when a limited-overs match cannot be completed due to weather/conditions and no valid result can be calculated).
