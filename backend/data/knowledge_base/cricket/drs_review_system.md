# DRS (Decision Review System)

## What DRS is

The Decision Review System (DRS) lets teams challenge an on-field umpire's decision using technology: ball-tracking (e.g. Hawk-Eye), UltraEdge/Snickometer (edge detection), and replays. It is used in international cricket and many major domestic competitions, though not at all levels of the game (it is a playing condition, not a core Law).

## Number of reviews

- In Test cricket, each team gets 2 unsuccessful reviews per innings.
- In ODIs and T20Is, each team typically gets 1 unsuccessful review per innings.
- A review is only "used up" if it is unsuccessful (the on-field decision stands). If the on-field decision is overturned, the team keeps its review.
- "Umpire's call" outcomes do not cost the reviewing team their review, because the on-field decision stands but it isn't counted as a wrong review either.

## How a review is requested

- Only the batter on strike (for their own dismissal) or the fielding captain can request a review.
- The request must be made quickly (typically within 15 seconds of the on-field umpire's decision), signalled with the "TV screen" hand gesture.
- Players cannot consult with the dressing room/coach before deciding to review (except for checking whether a no-ball occurred, which is now often checked automatically).

## What can be reviewed

- Caught behind / bat-pad catches (using UltraEdge to check for bat contact).
- LBW decisions (using ball-tracking to check pitching, impact, and projected path to the stumps).
- Stumpings and run-outs (using replay/TV umpire, which does not use up a review — these can also be referred directly by the on-field umpire).
- Boundary calls (whether the fielder touched the rope while in contact with the ball).

## Umpire's call

For LBW reviews specifically, ball-tracking shows a projection of the ball's path after impact. If the projection is close to the stumps (generally, less than roughly half the ball is shown hitting the stumps, or the impact/pitching location is very marginal), the result is "umpire's call" — meaning the on-field decision (out or not out) stands. This exists because ball-tracking has a margin of error, and the on-field umpire's original judgement is given the benefit of the doubt in marginal cases.

## No-ball checks

In many competitions, a front-foot no-ball check is automatically done by the third umpire for every delivery that results in a wicket, before the on-field decision is confirmed, independent of the team review system.
