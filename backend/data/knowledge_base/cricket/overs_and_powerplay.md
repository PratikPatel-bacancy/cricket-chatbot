# Overs, Powerplays, and Over-Rate Rules

## What an over is

An over consists of six legal deliveries bowled consecutively by one bowler from one end. No-balls and wides do not count toward the six and must be re-bowled. A bowler cannot bowl two overs in a row (a different bowler, or the same bowler from the other end after another bowler has had a turn, must be used).

## Powerplays in limited-overs cricket

Powerplays are periods of an innings with fielding restrictions, designed to encourage attacking batting.

### ODI powerplays
- **Powerplay 1 (overs 1–10)**: Maximum of 2 fielders allowed outside the 30-yard circle.
- **Powerplay 2 (overs 11–40, "middle overs")**: Maximum of 4 fielders allowed outside the circle.
- **Powerplay 3 (overs 41–50)**: Maximum of 5 fielders allowed outside the circle.

### T20 powerplay
- **Powerplay (overs 1–6)**: Maximum of 2 fielders allowed outside the 30-yard circle.
- After the powerplay, up to 5 fielders are allowed outside the circle for the remaining overs.

## Over-rate rules

Teams are required to bowl their overs within a set time to keep the match on schedule. Persistent slow over-rates can result in:

- Fines for the fielding captain and team.
- In some T20 competitions, a mandatory extra fielder inside the 30-yard circle for the remaining overs if the fielding side falls behind the required over-rate ("fielding restriction" penalty).
- In Test cricket, minimum over-rate requirements per day, with points/fine penalties applied by the governing body (e.g. ICC World Test Championship points deductions for slow over-rates).

## Free hit

In many limited-overs formats, a no-ball is followed by a "free hit" delivery, on which the batter cannot be dismissed by any method except run out, regardless of what would normally be out.

## Death overs / powerplay strategy terms

- **Death overs**: The final few overs of a limited-overs innings (commonly overs 16–20 in T20, or 41–50 in ODIs), typically associated with attacking batting and defensive bowling variations (yorkers, slower balls).
- **Middle overs**: The overs between the powerplay and the death overs, generally considered a consolidation phase for the batting side.
