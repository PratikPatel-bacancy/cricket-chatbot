# LBW (Leg Before Wicket)

## What LBW means

LBW (Leg Before Wicket) is a way a batter can be dismissed if the ball, delivered by the bowler, hits the batter's body (usually the leg or pad) instead of the bat, and would otherwise have gone on to hit the stumps. It is Law 36 of the Laws of Cricket (MCC).

## The five conditions for LBW

For an LBW dismissal to be given, umpires check five conditions, all of which must be true:

1. **Legal delivery**: The ball must not be a no-ball. A batter cannot be out LBW off a no-ball.
2. **Pitching**: The ball must pitch either in line with the stumps, or outside the batter's off stump. If the ball pitches outside leg stump, the batter cannot be given out LBW, even if it would have hit the stumps.
3. **Impact**: The ball must strike the batter's body (pad, leg, or other part, excluding the hand holding the bat) in line between wicket and wicket (i.e., between the imaginary line of off stump and leg stump) — UNLESS the batter was not attempting to play a shot, in which case impact outside the line of off stump can still be out.
4. **Would have hit the stumps**: The ball, after impact, must be going on to hit the stumps (umpire's judgement, or ball-tracking technology under DRS).
5. **No inside edge**: If the batter hits the ball with the bat first (an inside edge) before it strikes the pad, the batter cannot be out LBW.

## Quick decision guide

- Pitched outside leg stump → Not out, regardless of anything else.
- Pitched in line or outside off, batter offering a shot, impact outside line of off stump → Not out.
- Pitched in line or outside off, batter NOT offering a shot, impact anywhere in line → Can be out if it would hit the stumps.
- Bat involved before pad → Not out LBW (though could be caught if a fielder takes the catch).

## Umpire's call

Under DRS (Decision Review System), if ball-tracking shows the ball would only clip the stumps (in the "umpire's call" zone, generally where less than 50% of the ball is hitting the stumps based on the tracking margin), the on-field umpire's original decision stands even on review.
