# VAR and Officiating

## What VAR is

The Video Assistant Referee (VAR) is an additional match official, supported by a video review
team, who can review the on-field referee's decisions using video replays for a limited set of
situations, to correct "clear and obvious errors" or serious missed incidents.

## What VAR can review

VAR is used only for four categories of decision:

1. **Goals** — checking for an offense in the build-up (e.g. offside, a foul, or the ball going
   out of play) before a goal is scored.
2. **Penalty decisions** — whether a penalty should or shouldn't have been awarded.
3. **Direct red card incidents** — mistaken identity or clear and obvious red-card offenses missed
   or wrongly given.
4. **Mistaken identity** — the referee cautioning or sending off the wrong player.

## How a VAR review happens

The referee can be advised by the VAR team to review a decision, or can independently request a
review, either taking the video team's recommendation or going to a pitch-side monitor to view the
replay themselves before making a final decision. The on-field referee's decision is what counts —
VAR only recommends a review for clear and obvious errors, it does not overrule the referee
directly.

## Semi-automated offside technology

Many top competitions use tracking cameras and player/ball sensor data to draw an automated
offside line and detect the exact moment the ball is played, speeding up offside reviews and
improving accuracy compared to a human-drawn line on replay footage.

## Assistant referees and the fourth official

Two assistant referees patrol the touchlines, primarily helping judge offside and whether the ball
has left the field of play. The fourth official manages substitutions, added time, and technical
area conduct, and can also relay VAR information to the referee.
