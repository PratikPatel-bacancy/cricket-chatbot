# Offside

## The basic offside rule

A player is in an offside position if, at the moment the ball is played to them by a teammate,
they are nearer to the opponents' goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent
(usually the last outfield defender, since the goalkeeper is normally the last opponent). A player
is NOT offside if level with the second-last opponent or level with the last two opponents.

## Being in an offside position is not itself an offense

Merely standing in an offside position is not a foul. A player is only penalized for offside if,
at the moment a teammate plays the ball, they become involved in active play by:

- Interfering with play (e.g. touching the ball passed by a teammate).
- Interfering with an opponent (e.g. blocking their line of sight or challenging for the ball).
- Gaining an advantage from that position (e.g. playing a ball that rebounds off a post, crossbar,
  or an opponent, including the goalkeeper, having been in an offside position).

## Where offside does not apply

A player cannot be offside:

- From a goal kick.
- From a throw-in.
- From a corner kick.
- In their own half of the field.

## VAR and offside

In competitions using semi-automated offside technology or VAR, marginal offside calls are
reviewed using tracking data and virtual lines drawn through the attacker and the second-last
defender at the exact moment the ball is played, to determine whether any part of the attacker's
body that can legally play the ball was ahead of the defender.

## Consequence

If offside is called, play is restarted with an indirect free kick to the defending team from the
spot where the offside player became involved in active play.
